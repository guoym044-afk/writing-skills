# Language patterns

Use these as adaptable patterns, not text to paste without checking the evidence.

## Methods: overview and rationale

- "We conducted a `[design]` study to evaluate `[aim]`."
- "The study comprised `[number]` stages: `[stage list]`."
- "We selected `[method]` because it enabled `[relevant capability]`."
- "To minimize `[bias or interference]`, we `[control action]`."
- "We adapted `[cited method]` by `[specific modification]` to accommodate `[constraint]`."

## Methods: materials and procedures

- "`[Material/sample]` was obtained from `[source]` and used `[preparation status]`."
- "Measurements were acquired using `[instrument, model, manufacturer]` under `[critical conditions]`."
- "Before `[step]`, samples were `[preparation]`; they were then `[next action]`."
- "After `[criterion]` was met, `[action]` was performed."
- "All samples were assessed for `[quality criterion]` before analysis."

## Methods: data analysis

- "Raw data were `[preprocessing]` before `[analysis]`."
- "We quantified `[outcome]` as `[operational definition]`."
- "Differences between `[groups]` were evaluated using `[test]`, with `[assumptions/corrections]`."
- "Analyses were performed in `[software/version]`; `[threshold or interval]` was used when applicable."
- "Independent replicates were defined as `[definition]`."

## Results: orientation and navigation

- "To determine whether `[question]`, we `[brief method cue]`."
- "`[Primary finding]` (Figure/Table `[X]`)."
- "As shown in Figure `[X]`, `[key pattern]`."
- "The complete estimates are reported in Table `[X]`; the principal comparison was `[result]`."

## Results: quantitative findings

- "`[Outcome]` increased from `[value]` to `[value]` (`[effect/interval/test]`)."
- "Compared with `[reference group]`, `[group]` showed `[magnitude and direction]` in `[outcome]`."
- "No statistically supported difference was detected between `[groups]` (`[estimate/interval/p-value]`)."
- "`[Variable A]` was associated with `[variable B]` (`[coefficient and uncertainty]`)."
- "The model explained/predicted `[metric]` with `[performance value and uncertainty]`."

Do not use the "no difference" pattern as proof of equivalence unless an equivalence or non-inferiority design supports it.

## Results: comparisons and transitions

- "This pattern was consistent across `[conditions/subgroups]`."
- "In contrast, `[comparison condition]` showed `[different pattern]`."
- "The result agreed with `[model/control/preregistered prediction]` within `[stated tolerance]`."
- "Given this difference, we next tested whether `[next question]`."

## Calibrated evidence statements

Use stronger language only when the design and evidence justify it.

- Direct observation: "showed," "was higher/lower," "differed by," "was associated with."
- Evidence-near inference: "indicates," "suggests," "is consistent with."
- Causal statement: "caused," "led to," "resulted in" - reserve for designs that identify causation.

Avoid "proved," "demonstrated conclusively," "obviously," and "undoubtedly" unless a formal proof or exceptional evidentiary basis truly applies.

## Tense and voice decisions

- Completed study action: past tense - "We measured..." / "Samples were measured..."
- Observed finding: past tense - "The treatment reduced..."
- Figure/table as an artifact: present tense is often acceptable - "Figure 2 shows..."
- General relationship, equation, or algorithm: present tense - "The estimator updates..."
- Prior work with current relevance: present perfect only when that relationship matters - "Previous studies have used..."

Prefer active voice when it clarifies who made a choice or performed an analysis. Prefer passive voice when the object or procedure should occupy the topic position. Do not alternate voices randomly.
