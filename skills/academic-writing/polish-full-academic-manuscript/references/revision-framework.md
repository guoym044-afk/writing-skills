# Full-manuscript revision framework

## 1. Purpose, contribution, audience

- Make the research problem identifiable in the abstract and introduction.
- State the purpose and concrete contribution without inflated novelty claims.
- Align the title, abstract, final introduction paragraph, discussion, and conclusion.
- Remove institutional history or background that does not frame the argument.
- Define specialized terms and abbreviations at the audience-appropriate point.

## 2. Ideas, evidence, claims

- Connect every major claim to data, analysis, a cited source, or explicit reasoning.
- Verify that the evidence remains relevant if the central claim changes.
- Remove or relocate evidence that distracts from the main line of reasoning.
- Check whether the data support the strength and scope of the conclusion.
- Separate observation, interpretation, mechanism, implication, and speculation.
- Add concise interpretation after results when the manuscript merely reports them.
- State limitations and conflicting evidence where they change interpretation.

## 3. Overall structure

Use IMRaD/AIMRaD as functions:

- **Abstract**: What was studied, why, how, what was found, and what it means.
- **Introduction**: Current situation -> focused problem/gap -> study response -> contribution.
- **Methods**: Reproducible answer to the research question; no unexplained method-result mismatch.
- **Results**: Evidence in a logical order that matches the research questions or analyses.
- **Discussion**: Answer -> interpret -> compare -> qualify -> explain implications.
- **Conclusion**: Synthesize supported takeaways; avoid new evidence or broader claims than the study permits.

Respect the target journal's actual section requirements and disciplinary variants.

## 4. Useful organizational patterns

### General to specific

Move from a broad definition, generalization, comparison, or purpose statement to the narrower issue. Widen only when the paragraph or section has earned a broader implication.

### Situation-problem-solution-evaluation

Use this sequence for a research rationale, introduction passage, or applied discussion:

1. Establish the situation.
2. Identify the precise problem.
3. Present the study's response or solution.
4. Evaluate its significance, applicability, or limits.

### Paragraph evidence structure

Use a flexible hourglass or “topic-evidence-analysis” pattern:

1. Introduce the paragraph's key term and purpose.
2. Present the most detailed evidence, comparison, or reasoning.
3. Add concise interpretation, limitation, implication, or transition when useful.

Do not force a concluding summary sentence if it merely repeats the topic sentence.

## 5. Structural audit questions

- Can a reader state the paper's question and contribution after reading the abstract?
- Does each section perform a distinct function?
- Does every paragraph advance the section's purpose?
- Are research questions, methods, results, and discussion aligned one-to-one where appropriate?
- Are headings informative and ordered logically?
- Are repeated arguments consolidated?
- Are gaps in logic, data coverage, or presentation explicit?
- Does the conclusion answer the stated question at the supported level of certainty?
