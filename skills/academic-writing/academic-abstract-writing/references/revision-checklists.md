## 1. Evidence ledger

Before revising claims, map each abstract statement to evidence in the supplied paper or notes.

| Abstract claim | Source location | Evidence status | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Claim or number | Section/table/figure/page | Direct / inferred / missing | Keep / qualify / remove / query |

Rules:

- Preserve quantities, comparators, directions, uncertainty, and limitations.
- Do not upgrade association to causation.
- Do not call a method novel, first, superior, robust, efficient, or general unless evidence and scope support the word.
- If source evidence is missing, insert a visible placeholder or ask; do not guess.

## 2. Move audit

Label each sentence with one primary move: B (background), G (gap), O (objective), M (method), R (result), C (conclusion), or I (implication).

Check:

- Is every required move present for the selected genre?
- Does each sentence have a clear job?
- Is the objective answered by the results?
- Does the conclusion follow from the reported results?
- Does the abstract mirror the paper rather than introducing new material?
- Are methods and results sufficiently specific for primary research?
- Is a review abstract synthesizing the field rather than imitating an experiment?

## 3. Sentence-level audit

For each sentence, check:

- Put the main actor and action early.
- Prefer one main claim per sentence.
- Replace vague nouns and verbs with field-specific terms.
- Use consistent terminology for the same concept.
- Keep tense aligned with rhetorical function.
- Use active or passive voice deliberately, not mechanically.
- Define an essential nonstandard abbreviation once; delete nonessential abbreviations.
- Remove throat-clearing such as “It is well known that” and empty claims such as “very important.”
- Remove citations unless the venue allows or requires them.
- Avoid beginning a sentence with a numeral when venue rules prohibit it.

## 4. Compression pass

Cut in this order:

1. Generic background the target audience already knows.
2. Repeated statements of the purpose.
3. Literature detail and citations not required in the abstract.
4. Procedural detail that does not establish validity or distinguish the approach.
5. Secondary results that do not support the main conclusion.
6. Redundant transitions, nominalizations, and stacked modifiers.

Protect:

- The precise gap or problem.
- The central objective.
- The method details needed to judge credibility.
- The principal result, preferably with concrete evidence.
- The conclusion's scope and qualification.

After each cut, recount using the venue's definition. Some systems count characters, exclude spaces, or count an image against the limit.

## 5. Venue-compliance pass

Confirm:

- Exact word or character limit.
- Required section headings and order.
- Title and keywords rules.
- Citation, abbreviation, figure, table, and reference rules.
- Whether author details count toward the limit.
- Required English variety or terminology style.
- Whether study registration, funding, ethics, trial identifiers, or reporting-guideline items are required.

Do not assume an example venue's rules apply elsewhere.

## 6. Genre-specific checks

### Primary research

- Report actual sample/data and core analytical method when available.
- Include at least one central, specific result.
- Match each conclusion to a reported result.
- Avoid a conclusion that merely repeats the objective.

### Narrative review

- State scope and organizing question.
- Synthesize themes, agreements, disagreements, or gaps.
- Avoid isolated uncited numbers.
- End with a field-level implication or recommendation.

### Systematic review or meta-analysis

- Include search/selection scope and study count when required.
- Report central synthesis or pooled estimate and uncertainty when available.
- Reflect evidence quality and heterogeneity in the conclusion.

### Conference abstract

- Make theme relevance explicit and genuine.
- Ensure the submission stands alone without the paper.
- Distinguish completed, preliminary, ongoing, and proposed work.
- Give reviewers a concrete reason the audience will benefit.

### Extended abstract

- Follow the provided template and page limit.
- Keep figures/tables readable and permitted.
- Ensure references and visuals count correctly toward the limit.

## 7. Translation checks

- Preserve all claims, qualifiers, quantities, units, signs, and comparisons.
- Preserve move order unless the target venue requires a different structure.
- Use established disciplinary terminology, not literal calques.
- Rebuild long source-language sentences into idiomatic target-language syntax.
- Recount words or characters after translation.
- Flag ambiguous source wording instead of silently choosing a stronger meaning.

## 8. Recommended output formats

### Finalization

Provide:

1. Final abstract.
2. Word count and, when relevant, character count.
3. Remaining assumptions, placeholders, or compliance risks.

### Diagnostic review

Provide:

1. Overall verdict.
2. Move map by sentence.
3. High-priority evidence or structure problems.
4. Concision and language problems.
5. A revised version if authorized.

### Before-and-after revision

Provide the final text first, then a compact list of material changes. Do not overwhelm the user with every copyedit unless requested.
