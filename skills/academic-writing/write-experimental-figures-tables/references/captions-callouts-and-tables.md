# Captions, callouts, and table text

## Generic placement conventions

Target-journal instructions override these defaults.

- Figure: place the title and legend below the figure.
- Table: place the title above the table and explanatory notes below it.
- Use a concise, informative title. A generic title such as "Comparison results" is insufficient.
- A figure legend uses complete sentences for method and interpretation-neutral description.
- A table usually relies on its title, headings, and notes rather than a narrative figure-style legend.

## Figure legend content

Write the shortest legend that makes the figure independently interpretable. Include only what applies:

1. **Title/headline**: objective, method, comparison, or overall result.
2. **Panel map**: explain panels `(A)`, `(B)`, and so on in order.
3. **System and conditions**: samples, groups, intervention, time, dose, or model.
4. **Measurement and summary**: what was measured and how values were summarized.
5. **Encoding**: colors, symbols, lines, shaded regions, images, and scale bars.
6. **Statistics**: `n`, biological/technical replicate distinction, error bars, test, comparison set, correction, exact or threshold p-value notation.
7. **Provenance**: citation, adaptation/reproduction statement, and permission or license when necessary.

Do not use the legend to argue the broad meaning of the finding when the manuscript has a separate Discussion. Describe what elements mean and how the experiment was conducted.

### Panel template

`Figure X. [Informative title]. (A) [Sample/condition and measurement]. (B) [Second analysis and encoding]. Points represent [unit]; lines show [summary/model], and shading denotes [interval]. Data are [summary] from [n and replicate type]. Statistical comparisons used [test and correction]; [symbol definitions].`

## Table title and notes

Use a title that identifies the population/system, variables, and comparison. Then make headings self-explanatory.

Order notes as needed:

1. abbreviation definitions;
2. value format, such as mean (SD), median [IQR], or estimate (95% CI);
3. sample sizes or missing-data rules;
4. model adjustments;
5. statistical tests and symbol definitions;
6. source citations.

## In-text callouts

Call out every figure and table in numerical order. Combine navigation with a finding or method detail rather than writing a location-only sentence.

Preferred patterns:

- `As shown in Fig. 2A, [headline result].`
- `[Headline result] (Fig. 2A).`
- `Figure 2B shows that [specific observation].`
- `[Method or grouping] yielded [result] (Table 1).`

Use a standalone location sentence only when it improves a complex transition. Vary integrated and parenthetical placements to maintain flow.

## Cross-reference audit

- Match `Figure`, `Fig.`, `Table`, and panel formatting to the journal.
- Verify number, panel letter, capitalization, and order of first mention.
- Confirm that the callout's claim is actually visible or statistically supported in that panel.
- Avoid pointing to the whole multi-panel figure when only one panel supports the sentence.
- Do not duplicate the full legend or list all values in the prose.
