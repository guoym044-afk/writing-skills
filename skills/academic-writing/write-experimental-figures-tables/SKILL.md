# Write Experimental Figures and Tables

## Core workflow

1. Identify the requested deliverable and keep the response within scope: visual choice, title, caption/legend, table notes, in-text callout, Results paragraph, full Results subsection, or audit.
2. Inspect every supplied source: visual, table, raw values, statistical output, draft prose, target-journal instructions, and nearby manuscript context. If an image or PDF is supplied, read labels, units, panels, error bars, sample sizes, and significance markers before drafting.
3. Establish the evidence boundary. List internally what is observed, calculated, statistically tested, inferred, and unknown. Never invent values, tests, significance, sample sizes, mechanisms, or causal relations.
4. Determine the communication goal: exact values, overall trend, distribution, relationship, process, method, spatial pattern, or multi-study comparison. Select or critique the visual accordingly.
5. Draft the minimum complete text. Mention only representative values and the comparisons needed for the claim; do not narrate every plotted point or repeat the caption in the Results prose.
6. Verify all numbers, directions, group labels, units, panels, statistics, and cross-references against the source.
7. Apply the target journal's author instructions when available. Otherwise, state any material convention assumed and use the generic conventions in the references.

## Evidence and calculation rules

- Distinguish direct reading from calculation. Label a computed comparison when it is not printed in the source.
- Use absolute difference for same-unit separation: `B - A`.
- Use percentage-point difference for two percentages: `B% - A%` percentage points.
- Use relative change only with a nonzero, meaningful baseline: `(B - A) / A * 100%`.
- Use ratio or fold difference as `B / A`; prefer "B was 2.1 times A" or "B was 2.1-fold higher than A" only when the intended convention is clear. Avoid ambiguous fold wording when precision matters.
- Preserve reported precision unless the user requests harmonization. Do not create extra significant digits.
- Report statistical significance only when a valid test result or marked comparison is supplied. A visible difference, non-overlapping error bars, or trend line alone is not proof of significance.
- Treat error bars according to their stated definition; do not assume SD, SE, or CI.
- Use association language for observational evidence. Reserve causal verbs for designs and analyses that support causation.
- If essential information is absent, draft with explicit placeholders such as `[n]`, `[test]`, or `[95% CI]`, then give a compact missing-information list.

## Results structure

Build each results unit around this sequence when the evidence supports it:

1. Purpose or comparison question.
2. Location plus headline finding, preferably in one sentence.
3. Representative quantitative evidence and statistical support.
4. Brief within-results interpretation at the level permitted by the manuscript structure.
5. Transition to the next experiment or figure.

For a longer section, order visuals to answer the research aims rather than to reproduce experimental chronology. A useful progression is controls or context, primary finding, boundary conditions, and mechanism or robustness.

## Writing boundaries

- Keep Results concise, clear, and logically ordered; use one central claim per paragraph.
- In a separate Results section, emphasize what the data show and reserve broad mechanisms, literature debate, and implications for Discussion.
- In a combined Results and Discussion section, allow concise interpretation and comparison with prior work, but distinguish evidence from explanation.
- Integrate figure/table references with a finding whenever possible. Avoid empty navigation such as "Figure 2 is shown below."
- Call out figures and tables at least once, in numerical order, and use exact panel identifiers.
- Do not use `prove`, `confirm`, `demonstrate`, or `cause` when `suggest`, `support`, `is consistent with`, or `is associated with` better matches the evidence.

## Default response shape

Adapt to the request instead of forcing every section. For a typical drafting task, return:

1. **Recommended wording**: publication-ready English.
2. **Chinese note**: a brief explanation of the comparison logic when the user writes in Chinese.
3. **Evidence check**: only unresolved labels, calculations, statistics, or overclaim risks.

When several defensible phrasings exist, provide one preferred version and at most two concise alternatives with different emphasis.
