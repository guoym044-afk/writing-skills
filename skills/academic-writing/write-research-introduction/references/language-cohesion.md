## Cohesive chains

Repeat a small set of essential technical terms consistently. Use legitimate derivational forms when helpful, but do not replace a precise term merely to avoid repetition. A controlled chain such as `phenomenon -> current approaches -> limitation -> present method` makes the narrowing logic visible.

At paragraph boundaries, repeat or clearly refer to one key idea from the preceding paragraph before introducing the next level of specificity.

## Logical connectors

Choose connectors by relation:

- **Contrast or unexpected limitation:** however, nevertheless, despite, whereas.
- **Cause or consequence:** therefore, consequently, thus, because, resulting in.
- **Addition:** moreover, furthermore, additionally, also.
- **Sequence or ordered criteria:** first, second, finally, next.
- **Example or specification:** for example, specifically, in particular.

Do not place a connector at the start of every sentence. The semantic relation must exist even if the connector is removed.

## References

Use `this`, `these`, `such`, `those`, and comparative words only when the antecedent is unmistakable. Prefer a noun phrase such as `this limitation` or `these estimates` over an isolated `this` or `these`.

Use first person when permitted and when it identifies the authors' action more clearly than passive voice. Follow the target journal's convention.

## Tense and voice

- Use present tense for definitions, accepted facts, current relevance, and statements about what the paper addresses or shows.
- Use past tense for completed procedures and for reporting what an individual study did or found when the event is foregrounded.
- Use present perfect for a continuing research trend or accumulated body of work when no finished time is specified.
- Use cautious modality when evidence is limited: `may`, `might`, `can`, `suggests`, `is consistent with`.

Keep tense changes functional, not accidental. Avoid inflating certainty during translation or polishing.

## Functional sentence patterns

Adapt these patterns to the field; do not copy them mechanically or use placeholders in a final draft.

### Establish background

- `[Phenomenon] is central to [specific outcome or problem].`
- `Recent work has focused on [approach] because [evidence-based reason].`
- `[Term] refers to [precise definition relevant to the study].`

### Synthesize prior work

- `Prior studies have used [approaches] to [shared aim], with [main established result].`
- `Whereas [approach A] offers [advantage], [approach B] is better suited to [condition].`
- `Evidence from [context] indicates [finding], although [boundary or uncertainty].`

### Establish the niche

- `However, it remains unclear whether [specific unknown] under [condition].`
- `Existing approaches are limited by [supported limitation], which prevents [consequence].`
- `Despite [established advance], [specific question] has not been resolved.`
- `This discrepancy leaves open the question of [question].`

### Occupy the niche

- `To address this gap, this study [tests/develops/quantifies/compares] [object] in [scope].`
- `We hypothesized that [testable prediction] because [reason].`
- `Using [design/method], we examined [variables or mechanism].`
- `This work contributes [specific capability or evidence] for [bounded use].`

### Provide organization when required

- `Section 2 [function]; Section 3 [function]; and Section 4 [function].`

Prefer semantic functions (`develops`, `evaluates`, `tests`) over empty announcements (`talks about`, `introduces some information`).

## Concision and clarity

- Give each sentence one main job.
- Put the grammatical subject and verb near each other.
- Replace stacked noun phrases with clear verbs when possible.
- Use infinitive and prepositional phrases only when they shorten rather than obscure the sentence.
- Mix short orientation sentences with longer sentences that integrate evidence.
- Define abbreviations at first use and avoid unnecessary synonyms for key concepts.
- Remove promotional adjectives such as `groundbreaking`, `remarkable`, or `unprecedented` unless independently justified.
- Preserve numeric units, comparison groups, populations, and boundary conditions during revision.
