# Source-derived design notes

This skill was synthesized primarily from two user-provided PDFs. These notes preserve the provenance of the workflow without requiring the original scanned documents during routine use.

## `introduction.pdf` (2 pages)

Key source points:

- An Introduction reviews relevant research and narrows to the reason for the present study.
- Definitions or statistics may establish relevance when appropriate.
- The final one or two paragraphs should signal the gap or need, justify the study, and state the present objective.
- Hypotheses and brief methods may accompany the objective.
- Length and citation volume vary by article and journal.
- Reviews support established background; primary research supports specific data; authoritative reports may support official statistics.
- Present tense predominates for established facts.

## `重点学习Introduction.pdf` (20 pages, Chapter 4)

Key source points:

- The Introduction provides the paper's rationale and moves from general discussion to the particular research question while attracting reader interest.
- Read the target journal's instructions and field papers before drafting because components and length vary.
- A four-part model establishes field importance/background, describes prior research, locates a gap, and describes the present paper.
- A related five-rule model includes the problem's nature and scope, pertinent literature, method and its rationale, principal results or research questions, and optional conclusions or paper organization.
- Examples demonstrate short, standard, and detailed Introductions, all using broad-to-narrow organization.
- Paragraph topic sentences, evidence, short/long sentence variation, lexical repetition, conjunctions, and demonstrative or personal references create cohesion.
- Effective writing should be accurate, brief, and clear. Writers may imitate macro structure, linking devices, idiomatic expressions, and syntax patterns from strong papers, but should not copy entire sentences.
- Drafting the Introduction after Methods or Results can help ensure that the stated objective, method, and findings reflect the completed study.
- A strong Introduction combines clear background, relevant citations, methods where appropriate, a research gap, and the study's distinct significance.

## Synthesis decisions

The skill unifies the sources into a flexible four-move model rather than imposing a fixed paragraph count. It treats methods, results, conclusions, hypotheses, contributions, and paper organization as venue-dependent options. It adds explicit evidence-integrity checks so that stylistic revision cannot create unsupported claims or fabricated citations.
